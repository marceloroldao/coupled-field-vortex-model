# Electromagnetic / Gauge Limit

## Status

`known-limit` for the Maxwell gauge sector; `candidate` for any stronger claim that the full coupled model simplifies electromagnetism.

## Gauge completion

Starting from the complex scalar field,

\[
\psi=\sqrt{\rho}\,e^{i\Theta/2},
\]

introduce the Abelian gauge field through

\[
D_\mu=\partial_\mu-iqA_\mu,
\qquad
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu.
\]

The gauge-completed action contains

\[
|D_\mu\psi|^2-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}.
\]

Written in amplitude-phase variables, the phase appears only through a gauge-invariant combination of the form

\[
\partial_\mu\Theta-2qA_\mu.
\]

This is the natural bridge between the master variables and an Abelian electromagnetic sector.

## Maxwell recovery

Varying the action with respect to \(A_\mu\) gives a Maxwell-type equation

\[
\partial_\nu F^{\nu\mu}=J^\mu,
\]

with the matter current determined by the scalar amplitude and gauge-invariant phase gradient. Schematically,

\[
J^\mu\propto q\rho\left(\partial^\mu\Theta-2qA^\mu\right).
\]

The exact normalization depends on the convention chosen for the phase factor and kinetic term.

## What is established

The Abelian gauge construction and Maxwell recovery are standard field theory. They are therefore tagged `known-limit`, not novelty.

## What must be tested

The research question is whether the coupled \((\rho,\Theta,\chi)\) structure gives a simpler or more useful effective representation for:

- current transport and phase-coherent media;
- vortex electrodynamics;
- mixed scalar/gauge modes;
- regimes analogous to superconducting or multicomponent Ginzburg-Landau systems;
- numerical reduction of coupled electromagnetic problems.

Any claim that electric and magnetic phenomena are generated by one additional geometry must be derived from the action and compared quantitatively with Maxwell theory before promotion beyond `candidate`.

## Numerical validation targets

1. Recover vacuum wave propagation with the expected dispersion relation.
2. Recover charge/current conservation.
3. Verify gauge invariance numerically.
4. Test static Coulomb-like configurations where appropriate.
5. Compare vortex flux profiles with Abelian-Higgs / Ginzburg-Landau benchmarks.
